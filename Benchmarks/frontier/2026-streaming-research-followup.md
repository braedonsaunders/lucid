# Streaming research follow-up — September 5, 2026

The [August 31 revision of Stream-DiffVSR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23709v3) uses past-only motion guidance, a four-step denoiser and a temporal decoder. Its authors report 328 ms per 720p frame on an RTX 4090. That is a useful low-latency diffusion result in its comparison class, but exceeds Lucid's entire 50 ms delivery budget before any Mac port. It is a possible teacher research reference, not a measured native replacement. The original submission was December 2025; the revision date must not be presented as a new September architecture.

[CVPR 2026 CDA-VSR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07694) uses codec motion vectors and residual maps to align and combine previous-frame features. The [author release](https://github.com/sspBIT/CDA-VSR) was inspected at commit `5630821e5df4e110878d0caae39bcc53f658e9a9`; its released checkpoint and source hashes are in the adjacent receipt. The implementation's forward signature requires video, motion vectors and residual maps, and imports MMCV's modulated deformable convolution. Lucid's current browser capture sends decoded VideoFrame pixels and color/timestamp metadata (`BrowserExtension/content.js`), not these codec priors. Inference: adopting the release would require a new input route or separately measured prior estimation and a native operator implementation. Supplying fake zero metadata would not reproduce its published method. No upstream code or weights are integrated, and no GPU comparison is claimed.

The [September 2 perceptual-regularization paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02016v1) adds VGG feature supervision to SR3, with MRI and facial-image experiments. Its inference procedure remains unchanged. This verifies a September technique but supplies no evidence of real-time compressed-video or native macOS performance. Lucid has already measured perceptual-supervision tradeoffs; publication recency alone is not a reason to repeat that experiment.

The quantized 2× presentation shortcut subsequently failed the wide regression screen and remains rejected. The corrected full 4× tensor output now matches shipping in 960 native packet comparisons; its separate shared-storage optimization is documented in the tensor-output reports. The coarse local controller completed its fixed 8,000-step RTX 4080 run, but its roughly 1% perceptual gains miss the unchanged 3% gates on both development screens; see [completed results](spatial-conditioning-protocol.md). A separate [past-information probe](past-information-protocol.md) also fails its declared mechanism gate. Neither native cost admission nor exact output conversion establishes a quality breakthrough. Flicker tuning remains deferred.

## Additional primary-source checks, September 5

[QuADA-GS, June 28](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29400v1), adaptively allocates Gaussians for arbitrary-scale image reconstruction. Its Table 3 reports 349 ± 79 ms at 4× on 720×720 DIV2K crops with an RDN encoder. The hardware was not established in this inspection, so this is not a hardware-normalized comparison. Its routing ideas merit consideration; the published table does not establish Lucid's real-time budget.

[PixelSR, August 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00646v1), caches predictions for identical local RGB patches and uses nearest-neighbor reconstruction for constant backgrounds. The paper explicitly distinguishes screen content from natural images: the latter have much less exact repetition. Inference: this could suit a screen-content mode, but cannot justify a general compressed-video speed claim without measuring hit rates and total cache overhead on Lucid inputs. No implementation or checkpoint has been admitted.

[IQ-LUT, April 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07000v1), combines residual learning, interpolated low-bit lookup tables and nonuniform quantization with distillation. Table 1 compares PSNR/SSIM on image SR datasets; it is not a perceptual-video or native Metal benchmark. It supplies a possible compact student architecture, not evidence that lookup-table conversion would preserve our shipping network or quality gates.

[Ultra Flash, June 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09150v1), reports approximately 30 fps at 960×1664 on B200 in its main results. Its pipeline generates video from a Wan-based latent model; it is not a drop-in restoration model accepting Lucid's decoded frames. The appendix also labels a 30.2 fps table as H200 despite the main text distinguishing about 17 fps on H200. Treat those hardware/throughput claims as unresolved author-reported figures, not a reproducible competitive baseline. No model download or training was started from this inspection.

These checks supplement the measured NVIDIA VFX baseline and existing 2026 model experiments. Recency alone does not authorize replacing a measured model, weakening a gate, or describing an adaptation as novel.
